Why I bake this way

I started making gluten-free desserts for my nana and one of my close friends because they're two of the people I care about most. I noticed that a lot of the time when there are desserts or treats around, they don't get to enjoy the same things everyone else does because of their gluten intolerance. That never felt fair to me, so I wanted to make something that they could eat without worrying and still think tastes really good.

Almond Flour Chocolate Chip Cookies

A soft-centered, crackle-topped gluten-free sugar cookie, built on almond flour and finished with torn squares of milk or dark chocolate for pockets of melt in every bite.

2h 15m Prep + Chill
14m Bake
20–24 Cookies
177°C Oven

Ingredients

Before you start: use fine, blanched almond flour — not almond meal — for a smooth, non-gritty crumb. The dough needs at least 2 hours in the fridge, so plan ahead.
Dry base
  • 288g super-fine blanched almond flour, spooned and leveled
  • 30g cornstarch (or tapioca starch)
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp baking soda
  • 1/4 tsp fine salt
Wet mixture
  • 113g unsalted butter, melted and slightly cooled
  • 100g granulated sugar
  • 50g packed brown sugar, light or dark
  • 1 large egg, room temperature
  • 2 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 1/2 tsp almond extract
Mix-ins & finishing
  • 150g milk or dark chocolate, cut into small squares added
  • 100g granulated sugar, for rolling

Instructions

8 steps
  1. 1

    Whisk the almond flour, cornstarch, baking powder, baking soda, and salt together in a medium bowl. Set it aside.

  2. 2

    In a large bowl, whisk the melted butter with both sugars until smooth. Add the egg, vanilla, and almond extract and whisk again until fully combined.

  3. 3

    Add the dry mixture to the wet mixture and stir with a spatula until no streaks remain. Fold in the 150g of chopped milk or dark chocolate, distributing the pieces evenly through the dough. The dough will feel quite soft — that's expected.

  4. 4

    Cover the bowl and chill the dough so it firms up enough to shape.

    ⏱ 2 hr minimum (up to 3 days)
  5. 5

    Preheat the oven and line two baking sheets with parchment. Portion the chilled dough into balls of about 28–30g each, roll each one in granulated sugar, and space them 3 inches apart on the sheets.

    ⏱ preheat to 177°C
  6. 6

    Use the flat bottom of a measuring cup to gently press each ball down slightly — these cookies won't spread much on their own.

  7. 7

    Bake until the tops look crackled and the edges have set but the centers still look a little soft. Cool on the sheet before moving to a rack.

    ⏱ 13–15 min bake · 10–20 min rest
  8. 8

    Store cooled cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 5 days.

Notes

Chocolate choice

Milk chocolate gives a sweeter, softer bite; dark chocolate (around 60–70%) plays nicely against the sweet dough. Chopping your own chips by hand gives irregular pieces that melt unevenly in a good way.

Almond flour matters

Almond meal (made with the skins on) will make these gritty and dense. Look for "fine" or "super-fine" blanched almond flour specifically.

Make ahead

Dough keeps in the fridge up to 3 days, or roll into balls and freeze for up to 3 months — thaw for 30 minutes before rolling in sugar and baking.

Dairy-free swap

Refined coconut oil or vegan butter can replace the butter; expect a slightly less chewy texture.

Gluten-Free Lemon Blackberry Cake

A moist almond and quinoa flour cake, bright with lemon and honey, finished with a lemon glaze and a spoonful of mashed blackberries in the center.

15m Prep
40m Bake
9" cake Yield
177°C Oven

Ingredients

Before you start: you'll need a 9-inch springform pan, lined on the bottom with parchment. Zest your lemons before juicing them.
Wet ingredients
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup olive oil
  • 1/3 cup honey
  • 1/4 cup lemon juice, about 2 lemons
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • Zest of 2 lemons
Dry ingredients
  • 2 cups almond flour
  • 1/2 cup quinoa flour
  • 1½ tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp baking soda
  • 1/4 tsp fine sea salt
Blackberry glaze
  • 1/4 cup frozen blackberries
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

Instructions

8 steps
  1. 1

    Line the bottom of a 9-inch springform pan with parchment, snap the pan back together, and trim the excess. Grease the sides and bottom with cooking spray.

    ⏱ preheat to 177°C
  2. 2

    Whisk the eggs and honey together until fluffy and pale yellow. Add the olive oil, lemon juice, and vanilla, and beat well to combine.

  3. 3

    Add the almond flour, quinoa flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt, and mix until just combined. Fold in the lemon zest.

  4. 4

    Transfer the batter to the prepared pan and bake for 20 minutes, then loosely cover with foil and continue baking until a skewer inserted in the center comes out clean.

    ⏱ 20 min, then 20 min more covered
  5. 5

    Let the cake cool completely in the pan before releasing the springform sides.

  6. 6

    Warm the frozen blackberries in a small saucepan over medium heat until soft enough to mash with a fork, then remove from the heat.

  7. 7

    Whisk the powdered sugar and lemon juice together into a glaze. Pour it over the cooled cake and spread it out with the back of a spoon. Spoon the mashed blackberries into the center and let the juices bleed into the glaze.

  8. 8

    Slice and serve — whipped cream, ice cream, fresh berries, or an extra drizzle of honey all work well alongside it.

Notes

Quinoa flour

It has a slightly nutty, earthy flavor that plays well against the lemon — an almond-flour-only version will taste noticeably sweeter and lighter.

Don't skip the foil

Almond flour cakes brown quickly. Tenting with foil partway through keeps the top from over-darkening before the center finishes baking.

Fully cool before glazing

Glazing a warm cake will make it slide straight off — wait until the cake is completely cool before releasing the pan and glazing.

Storage

Keep covered in the fridge for up to 4 days — the glaze stays glossy and the crumb stays moist.

Gluten-Free Brownies

Fudgy, chocolatey brownies made with a gluten-free flour blend — simple enough for a weeknight, and just as good the next day.

15m Prep
30-35m Bake
9 squares Yield
177°C Oven

Ingredients

Before you start: use a gluten-free all-purpose flour that already has xanthan gum blended in. For dairy-free, swap the butter for Smart Balance butter.
Batter
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted — dairy-free: use Smart Balance butter
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tbsp pure vanilla extract
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose gluten-free flour with xanthan gum (Pillsbury gluten-free works well)
  • 1/2 cup cocoa powder
  • 1/2 tsp baking soda
  • 1/2 tsp salt

Instructions

9 steps
  1. 1

    Preheat the oven.

    ⏱ preheat to 177°C
  2. 2

    Grease an 8×8 baking pan with gluten-free nonstick spray, or line it with parchment paper and coat the bottom and sides with spray or butter.

  3. 3

    In a large bowl, mix the melted butter, vanilla, granulated sugar, and brown sugar together until fully combined.

  4. 4

    Add the eggs one at a time, mixing until fully combined after each one.

  5. 5

    In a separate medium bowl, whisk together the gluten-free flour, cocoa powder, baking soda, and salt.

  6. 6

    Slowly add the dry mixture into the wet ingredients, mixing until smooth and fully combined.

  7. 7

    Pour the batter into the prepared 8×8 pan and spread it into an even layer.

  8. 8

    Bake until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out just barely clean and the sides start pulling away from the pan. Ovens vary, so keep an eye on it near the end.

    ⏱ 30–35 min
  9. 9

    Let the brownies cool completely before slicing. Store leftovers in an airtight container.
